01

One night without sleep, 68 brains

Each person was recorded resting for five minutes twice: after a normal night (NS) and after 24 to 30 hours awake (SD). We look at theta, a slow rhythm that tends to rise when we are sleepy, and ask who changes and how.

Everything lives on one screen. Follow it in this order.

The whole tool

02

Where does the rhythm change?

Each head is the scalp seen from above. Warm colors mean theta went up after the sleepless night, cool colors that it went down. A ring marks places where the change is unlikely to be chance.

The two rows separate two things: how much theta changed (amount) and how irregular it became (variability).

Where · what kind of change

03

Who gets hit, and does it show in behavior?

Every dot is a person. Left to right, theta became more unstable; bottom to top, they got slower on the reaction test. The amber corner holds people who got worse on both.

If instability went with slowness, the dots would line up along a diagonal. Here they do not.

Who · does it go together

04

Is it the same pattern in everyone?

Now one column per person and one row per electrode. If the vulnerable people shared a signature, their columns would look alike. Instead, red and blue are mixed: each person changes differently.

Same pattern, or one each

05

When, within the five minutes?

Press play in the Topography View and the whole tool moves through time: the maps evolve, a dot travels along this chart, the scatter and the heatmap recompute for the same 40-second window, and the similarity graph regroups.

When · does it build up

06

Does it depend on who the person is?

One control splits everyone in two: women and men, which night came first, good or poor sleepers. Every view repaints with two colors, so any difference shows up everywhere at once.

Who it depends on

07

Can we trust what we just saw?

The right-hand panel tries to break the finding: is it a real rhythm or background noise, could blinks explain it, do regions synchronize differently? Below them, a network links each person to the three whose pattern of change is most alike. The vulnerable people do not form a cluster.

Would it survive another analysis

08

So, what do we take home?

A sleepless night moves theta in different directions in different people, and that movement does not reliably tell who deteriorates. Close to 900 alternative analyses point the same way: none does better than chance. So the result asks for caution, and it points to where to look next.

The answer

Open full screen

Preliminary findings

  • Does everyone change the same way?39 and 29people more unstable and more stable without sleep; the mean barely rises (+0.00431).
  • Where does it change?0 of 61electrodes after FDR (4 uncorrected): a diffuse change, without a frontal focus.
  • When, within five minutes?63 of 6640-s windows with moving σθ in SD above NS (frontal).
  • Is more unstable slower?ρ = −0.04[−0.40, 0.32], n = 30: does not replicate the 0.544 of Cui et al. (2026).
  • Oscillation or 1/f?ρ = 0.82–0.89between the change in σθ and in periodic theta: the effect is oscillatory.
  • Does it depend on the analysis?p ≥ 0.09896 analyses, two preprocessing pipelines: no joint statistic exceeds chance.

Preliminary conclusion: without sleep, each person's theta moves in different directions, and that change does not robustly predict who deteriorates. The only hint is with mood (PANAS+), not with vigilance (PVT).

How to run the application

With a local server (recommended)

  1. Clone the repository or download it as a ZIP.
  2. From the repository root, serve the app folder: 
    cd deliveries/week12/app
python -m http.server 8000
  3. Open http://localhost:8000/ in the browser.

Without a server

Open deliveries/week12/app/index.html by double-click. The data are also bundled in data/data.js, so the app loads from disk.

Requirements: a current browser. Python (3.x) is only needed for the server option; there are no dependencies to install. Fonts load from Google Fonts and, offline, the system fonts are used.
