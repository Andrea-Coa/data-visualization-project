Summary

BATIMIX is an interactive visualization to explore whether a sleepless night changes each person's resting-EEG theta rhythm and whether that change anticipates who deteriorates. It uses the public ds004902 dataset: 68 people with EEG after normal sleep and after 24–30 hours without sleep. One view coordinates a vulnerability scatter, a person × electrode heatmap, scalp maps, the dynamics within five minutes and two secondary views (oscillatory versus aperiodic activity, and theta synchrony between regions); a multiverse of 896 analyses (two preprocessing pipelines, the main one with ICA) with permutation inference supports the robustness of the conclusions. The change is individual (39 people more unstable and 29 more stable), diffuse, oscillatory rather than 1/f, and does not predict slowing on the vigilance task (ρ = −0.10, n = 30). The only hint appears with mood.

Audience: sleep physiology researchers doing exploratory screening on public data. The tool helps decide which electrodes, regions and covariates deserve a formal test; it is neither diagnostic nor predictive.

Preliminary findings

  • Does everyone change the same way?39 and 29people more unstable and more stable without sleep; the mean barely rises (+0.00431).
  • Where does it change?0 of 61electrodes after FDR (4 uncorrected): a diffuse change, without a frontal focus.
  • When, within five minutes?63 of 6640-s windows with moving σθ in SD above NS (frontal).
  • Is more unstable slower?ρ = −0.04[−0.40, 0.32], n = 30: does not replicate the 0.544 of Cui et al. (2026).
  • Oscillation or 1/f?ρ = 0.82–0.89between the change in σθ and in periodic theta: the effect is oscillatory.
  • Does it depend on the analysis?p ≥ 0.09896 analyses, two preprocessing pipelines: no joint statistic exceeds chance.

Preliminary conclusion: without sleep, each person's theta moves in different directions, and that change does not robustly predict who deteriorates. The only hint is with mood (PANAS+), not with vigilance (PVT).

The application

A single header holds the title and all controls: region (frontal or centrotemporal), metric (σθ or μθ), group, Y-axis marker, Clear, Report and About, plus a chip with the pipeline (band, reference, epochs). Below, three contiguous columns, separated only by 1-px lines, fill the screen without scrolling. There is no instruction text: each panel title is its finding.

  • Scalp maps (center)2 × 3 matrix: NS, SD and Δ (SD minus NS) columns; σθ and μθ rows of the active group, with p and FDR marks. With exactly two people selected, the rows become those two people, with the header metric and shared scales.
  • Five minutes (center)Follows the header metric: with μθ, relative theta per 4-s epoch; with σθ, moving σθ in 40-s windows. NS versus SD, mean ± SE.
  • Vulnerability scatter (left)Frontal Δσθ against the Y-axis marker, with ρ and its n; the vulnerable quadrant is amber. The lasso defines a group and clicking points adds or removes people (1 to n); selected people are ringed.
  • Person × electrode heatmap (left)Two behavior-defined groups (deteriorate, resist) and their consistency C(e); selected people are outlined.
  • Secondary views (right, collapsible)Robustness checks that defend the main finding: «Oscillatory or 1/f?» (spectrum with 1/f fit, Δ exponent and offset, Δσθ versus periodic Δσθ) and «Driven by blinks?» (blink rate and Δσθ with and without blink windows). Exploratory: «θ synchrony between regions» (wPLI graph and effect per pair with 95% CI). Hovering any person shows their EEG quality.

How to run the application

With a local server (recommended)

  1. Clone the repository or download it as a ZIP.
  2. From the repository root, serve the app folder: 
    cd deliveries/week12/app
python -m http.server 8000
  3. Open http://localhost:8000/ in the browser.

Without a server

Open deliveries/week12/app/index.html by double-click. The data are also bundled in data/data.js, so the app loads from disk.

Requirements: a current browser. Python (3.x) is only needed for the server option; there are no dependencies to install. Fonts load from Google Fonts and, offline, the system fonts are used.
