01

One night without sleep, 68 brains

Each person was recorded resting for five minutes twice: after a normal night (NS) and after 24 to 30 hours awake (SD). We look at theta, a slow rhythm that tends to rise when we are sleepy, and ask who changes and how

Everything lives on one screen. Follow it in this order

The whole tool

02

Where does the rhythm change?

Each head is the scalp seen from above. Warm colors mean theta went up after the sleepless night, cool colors that it went down. A ring marks places where the change is unlikely to be chance

The two rows separate two things: how much theta changed (amount) and how irregular it became (variability).

Where · what kind of change

03

Who gets hit, and does it show in behavior?

Every dot is a person. Left to right, theta became more unstable; bottom to top, they got slower on the reaction test. The amber corner holds people who got worse on both

If instability went with slowness, the dots would line up along a diagonal. Here the trend is weak and not significant: centrotemporal ρ = +0.30, frontal ρ = +0.18.

Who · does it go together

04

Is it the same pattern in everyone?

Now one column per person and one row per electrode. If the vulnerable people shared a signature, their columns would look alike. Instead, red and blue are mixed: each person changes differently

Same pattern, or one each

05

When, within the five minutes?

Press play in the Topography View and the whole tool moves through time: the maps evolve, a dot travels along this chart, the scatter and the heatmap recompute for the same 40-second window, and the similarity graph regroups

When · does it build up

06

Does it depend on who the person is?

One control splits everyone in two: women and men, which night came first, good or poor sleepers. Every view repaints with two colors, so any difference shows up everywhere at once

Who it depends on

07

Can we trust what we just saw?

The right-hand panel tries to break the finding: is it a real rhythm or background noise, could blinks explain it, do regions synchronize differently? Below them, a network links each person to the three whose pattern of change is most alike. The vulnerable people do not form a cluster

Would it survive another analysis

08

Can a model tell the two nights apart?

Beyond looking, we asked a simple and explainable model (logistic regression) to recognize the sleepless night from the theta features alone. It reaches an AUC of 0.89 and picks the right night for 80% of the 68 people, and a permutation test gives p ≤ 0.001.

Can a model tell them apart

09

Where does the model go wrong?

Each of the 136 recordings is a point in a plane that summarizes all the features. Circles are normal sleep, diamonds are sleep deprivation, and an amber ring marks a mistake. A line joins the two nights of the same person

Where it errs

10

Which features push the decision?

Each dot is how strongly a feature pushes toward sleep deprivation (red) or toward normal sleep (blue), with its range across 50 models. Next to it, shuffling a whole family of features shows how much the model needs it

Why the model decides

11

Why did it decide that for this person?

Click a record and the panel shows what each feature contributed for that person on each night. Blue pulls toward normal sleep and red toward deprivation, so every decision can be inspected instead of trusted

One decision, explained

12

So, what do we take home?

A sleepless night moves theta in different directions in different people, and that movement does not reliably tell who deteriorates. Close to 900 alternative analyses point the same way: none does better than chance. A model still recognizes the sleepless night, so the nights do differ, but who deteriorates is hard to predict. So the result asks for caution, and it points to where to look next

The answer

Open full screen

Preliminary findings

  • Does everyone change the same way?46 and 22people more unstable and more stable without sleep; most rise (mean +0.0135, dz = 0.74), but not everyone
  • Where does it change?61 of 61electrodes after FDR for σθ (42 of 61 for μθ): a widespread change, across the whole scalp
  • When, within five minutes?61 of 6160-s windows with moving σθ in SD above NS (frontal): the rise holds throughout the five minutes
  • Is more unstable slower?ρ = +0.30[−0.07, +0.59], n = 30, p = 0.11, centrotemporal (frontal +0.18): the direction of Cui et al. (2026), weaker than their 0.54–0.56 and not significant
  • Oscillation or 1/f?ρ = 0.81–0.82between the change in σθ and in periodic theta: the effect is mostly oscillatory
  • Does it depend on the analysis?p ≥ 0.28896 analyses, two preprocessing pipelines: no joint statistic exceeds chance; PVT reaction time is the most consistent marker
  • Can a model recognize the night?AUC 0.89an explainable logistic model picks the sleepless night for 80% of the 68 people (permutation p ≤ 0.001), and every decision can be traced to its features

Preliminary conclusion: without sleep, theta becomes more unstable across the whole scalp in most people (about one in four goes the other way). How much it rises tracks slower reaction times only weakly: a group marker of sleep loss more than an individual marker of who deteriorates. (Figures recomputed on 15 November 2026 after correcting which session is normal sleep and which is deprivation.)

How to run the application

With a local server (recommended)

  1. Clone the repository or download it as a ZIP
  2. From the repository root, serve the app folder: 
    cd deliveries/week12/app
python -m http.server 8000
  3. Open http://localhost:8000/ in the browser

Without a server

Open deliveries/week12/app/index.html by double-click. The data are also bundled in data/data.js, so the app loads from disk

Requirements: a current browser. Python (3.x) is only needed for the server option; there are no dependencies to install. Fonts load from Google Fonts and, offline, the system fonts are used
