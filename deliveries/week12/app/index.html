About BATIMIX

Does the night-to-night change in resting theta show who deteriorates after sleep deprivation? Cui et al. (2026) reported that a rise in theta variability tracks PVT slowing (ρ ≈ 0.55, n = 28) on this dataset. BATIMIX re-examines it person by person. Exploratory screening, not diagnosis.

Key findings

  • Theta becomes more unstable: Δσθ frontal rises in 50 people and falls in 18; group t = 5.88, p < 0.001, dz = 0.71.
  • Widespread: 61 of 61 electrodes survive FDR for σθ, 45 of 61 for μθ.
  • Weakly linked to vigilance: Δσθ centrotemporal vs ΔPVT median RT ρ = +0.37 [+0.01, +0.64], n = 30 (frontal +0.16); with Cui's exact pipeline, ρ = +0.33 (p = 0.08).
  • Mostly oscillatory: Δσθ tracks periodic theta (ρ = 0.78; 1/f variability explains R² = 0.06); the aperiodic offset also rises after deprivation (dz = 0.48, p < 0.001), as Bai et al. (2024) reported.
  • Not blinks: removing blink windows leaves Δσθ unchanged (ρ = 0.95).

Data

OpenNeuro ds004902 (Xiang et al., 2024, CC0): 71 people after normal sleep and after 24–30 h awake, counterbalanced; eyes-open rest, 5 min, 61 electrodes, 500 Hz, no EOG.

Method

  • Preprocessing (tools/eeg_ica.py): 50 Hz notch, 1–40 Hz; bad channels interpolated; FCz re-inserted, average reference; extended Infomax ICA (30 components), ocular (Fp1/Fp2 correlation or ICLabel) and muscle (ICLabel p > 0.85) components removed (5.1 per recording); 75 × 4-s epochs, > 200 µV rejected (71 kept on average).
  • Metric: relative θ = 4–8 / 1–40 Hz (Welch) per epoch and electrode; σθ = SD across epochs, μθ = mean; Δ = deprivation − normal. Frontal (16) and centrotemporal (28) ROIs as in Cui et al.
  • Statistics: paired t per electrode with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR (61 tests); cluster-based permutation test (Maris and Oostenveld, 2007) as a global test of each Δ map; dz and 95% CI; Spearman ρ with 95% CI. Maps: v4 interpolation (EEGLAB, FieldTrip) on the real electrode positions.

Robustness

Multiverse of 896 analyses (2 pipelines, with and without ICA × 4 regions × 2 metrics × 2 change forms × 7 markers × 2 correlations × 2 outlier rules), with joint inference by 2000 permutations (Simonsohn et al., 2020): no joint statistic exceeds chance (σθ only: median r = +0.030, p = 0.34). The most consistent marker is PVT median RT (median r = +0.21 over its 128 analyses). All results: multiverse_specs.csv.

Limitations

PVT for only 30 people (power ≈ 0.35 for ρ = 0.3); public PVT summary columns, whereas Cui et al. likely recomputed median RT from trials; ICA components selected by rule, without visual review; no task EEG or event markers (no ERP).

How to read it

  1. Topography: where on the scalp theta changes, and whether it is the amount (μθ) or its fluctuation (σθ).
  2. Vulnerability: who changes most and whether that goes with doing worse on the behavioral marker.
  3. Individual: whether those people share one pattern or each changes differently.
  4. Time: when within the five minutes it happens; press play to follow it.
  5. Compare (header): whether it depends on sex, sleep quality or session order.
  6. Validation panel: whether the result survives other explanations, plus the similarity network between people.
